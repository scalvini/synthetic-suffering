# Readings on the case database page

> **Version:** 2.1  
> **Last updated:** 6 November 2026

## R1 Two regimes of qualification

Synthetic suffering appears in all eleven genres of the search brief, and 96 of the 113 cases lie outside humanitarian communication. The cases divide into two regimes by a single rule, which asks whether at least half of a genre's cases kept a disclosure when the image circulated. The qualified regime contains humanitarian communication, activist campaigns, victims' advocacy, documentary and true crime, health campaigns, advertising and forensic reconstruction. In this regime the producer discloses the synthesis in 50 of 65 cases, gives a justification in 54 and involves the people affected in 28. The unqualified regime contains war reporting, political communication, disaster communication and memorial communication. In this regime the image is presented as an authentic recording in 28 of 48 cases, a justification is given in 17 and the people affected take part in 2.

## R2 Political communication and the person depicted

Political communication belongs to the unqualified regime, since a disclosure accompanies only 2 of its 17 cases, although the producer is named in 14 of them and is a party, a government or a news organisation in 12. The genre therefore tests the responsibility of the producer where no qualification is offered. The producer is named in 57 of the 65 qualified cases against 27 of the 48 unqualified cases, so the description of humanitarian use as selective and heavily qualified extends to most genres whose producers put their name to the image, while the qualified genres differ in who is depicted. Humanitarian images depict no real person in all 17 cases, whereas the images of the other qualified genres depict real people in 34 of 48 cases.

## R3 Humanitarian cases over time

The humanitarian cases cluster in 2023, when 8 of the 17 were first published, and 8 of the 17 were followed by a policy or guideline consequence. After 2023 the humanitarian cases fall to 2 in 2024 and to 5 in 2025 and 2026 together. In those two years eight other genres have at least two cases each, namely war reporting, political communication, activist campaigns, memorial communication, victims' advocacy, documentary and true crime, health campaigns and forensic reconstruction. The record therefore supports both readings of the sector at once, since humanitarian organisations tested the technique in one year and then qualified or withdrew its uses. Production meanwhile moved to other genres and, within war reporting and disaster communication, to accounts that present the image as a recording.

## R4 Disclosure at origin and in circulation

The map places each case by the disclosure at origin and by the claim made about the image, so that qualification can be read as a position. The humanitarian cases occupy mainly the upper right, where a statement or a label accompanies an image that stands for a type or for an event without recordings, as 13 of the 17 do. The unqualified genres occupy mainly the bottom row and the first column, since 28 of their 48 cases were presented as authentic recordings and 33 carried no disclosure at origin. Ten cases carried a disclosure at origin and still circulated as authentic recordings, which shows that a disclosure does not travel with the image.

## R5 Debate in each regime

The debate differs between the regimes in kind more than in volume. A policy or guideline consequence followed 14 of the 65 qualified cases and 12 of the 48 unqualified cases, and a removal or a withdrawal is reported in 11 qualified and 12 unqualified cases. In the unqualified regime the debate consists largely of fact-checking, since fact-checking organisations examined 25 of its 48 cases against 8 of the 65 qualified cases. Six of the seven documented instances of the liar's dividend occur in the unqualified regime, where real images were dismissed as synthetic after synthetic images had circulated as real, and the remaining one (C32) belongs to activist campaigns.

## R6 The four questions of the joint paper

The notes relate each case to the four questions of the joint paper, and the counts show where each question has its material. The responsibility of the producer arises in 80 cases across every genre, whereas participation as remedy arises in 58 cases, 42 of them in the qualified regime. The distinction between evidentiary and appellative use arises in 48 cases, 9 of them in humanitarian communication, 11 in war reporting and disaster communication and 15 of the 17 in victims' advocacy and forensic reconstruction, where courts, police forces and families confront it directly. The type of synthesis relates the cases to the philosophical questions of the one-page summary. Depictions of generated and typified people raise the question of suffering without a sufferer, whereas depictions of real people raise the questions of consent and of the face.

## R7 The Global North and the Global South

The regions answer the question of the imbalance between the Global North and the Global South. The suffering depicted lies in the Middle East, Africa, Latin America or Asia in 44 cases. The producer is in the Global North (Europe, North America, Oceania or an international organisation) in 12 of them and in the regions depicted in 15, while in 17, the largest group, it could not be established. The imbalance in authorship is therefore hidden in the largest group, where the producer is anonymous, and among named producers those in the regions depicted outnumber those in the North.

## Coding notes

C01 and C02 are typed b although their descriptions state that the people depicted are fully generated. The type records what the person depicted stands for rather than how the image was made, and in both cases the person depicted stands for a group.

Disclosure in circulation is derived from two fields, since a case whose truth claim is 'presented as an authentic recording (undisclosed)' is recorded as undisclosed in circulation whatever its disclosure at origin.

Eight cases carry a year without a month and four carry no date. The timeline places the cases dated by year alone in the middle of that year as hollow marks, and it places the undated cases in a separate column.

The keyword rules for removal and apology are approximate, because the reception field is prose; the matched passages are readable in the record of each case, and the policy consequence comes from its own field.

The regime of a genre is a property of the sample, not of the genre. Activist campaigns moved from the unqualified to the qualified regime in the second round of searching, when five of their nine cases kept a disclosure in circulation, close to the threshold of half.
